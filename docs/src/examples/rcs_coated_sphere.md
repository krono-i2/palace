# Radar Cross Section of a Coated Sphere

!!! note

    The files for this example can be found in the
    [`examples/rcs_coated_sphere/`](https://github.com/awslabs/palace/blob/main/examples/rcs_coated_sphere)
    directory of the *Palace* source code.

In this example, we compute the radar cross section of a perfectly conducting sphere
covered by a lossy magnetic coating, illuminated by a plane wave, with the
[total-field/scattered-field formulation](../features/planewave.md). The layered sphere
has a closed-form solution, the Mie series, against which we validate the far field and
the radar cross section computed by *Palace*.

## Problem Setup

The sphere has a perfectly conducting (PEC) core of radius ``a = 5\text{ cm}`` and a
coating of outer radius ``b = 7.5\text{ cm}``, with relative permittivity
``\varepsilon_r = 4``, relative permeability ``\mu_r = 2`` and conductivity
``\sigma = 0.5\text{ S/m}``. It is illuminated by an x-polarized plane wave of amplitude
``E_0 = 1\text{ V/m}`` propagating along ``+z``, at ``600\text{ MHz}`` (wavelength
``\lambda = 0.5\text{ m}`` in vacuum, ``k b \approx 0.94``).

The domain is made of three nested cubes centered on the sphere (all lengths are
half-sides):

  - the total-field cube (``0.1\text{ m}``), whose surface ``S`` (the Huygens surface)
    injects the incident wave: the solution is the total field inside ``S`` and the
    scattered field outside;
  - the far-field cube (``0.18\text{ m}``), an interior surface outside of ``S`` on which
    the far field of the scattered field is computed;
  - the outer box (``0.3\text{ m}``), terminated by a second-order absorbing boundary.

The PEC core is a hole in the mesh, with a PEC boundary condition on its surface. The mesh
of second-order (curved) tetrahedra is generated by the Julia script `mesh/mesh.jl` with
[Gmsh](https://gmsh.info/), with an element size of ``\lambda / 10`` in vacuum and
``1.25\text{ cm}`` in the coating:

```bash
julia -e 'include("mesh/mesh.jl"); generate_rcs_coated_sphere_mesh(; filename="rcs_coated_sphere.msh"); generate_rcs_coated_sphere_mesh(; filename="rcs_empty.msh", empty=true)'
```

The second mesh, without the sphere, is used to measure the leakage of the plane wave
source. The figure below shows the geometry on the ``xz``-plane, with the attributes of
the mesh.

## Configuration File

The configuration file `rcs_coated_sphere.json` sets up a driven simulation at a single
frequency. The plane wave is defined in
[`config["Domains"]["PlaneWave"]`](../config/reference.md#config-domains), with the
volume attributes of the total-field region (the coating and the vacuum inside ``S``):

```json
"PlaneWave":
[
    {
        "Index": 1,
        "Attributes": [1, 2],
        "Direction": "+Z",
        "Polarization": "+X",
        "Amplitude": 1.0
    }
]
```

The coating is a material with `"Permittivity"`, `"Permeability"` and `"Conductivity"`,
the surface of the core is a `"PEC"` boundary, and the outer boundary is `"Absorbing"`
with `"Order": 2`. The far field is computed on the far-field cube, an interior surface,
on the E-plane (``\phi = 0``) and the H-plane (``\phi = 90^\circ``) every ``5^\circ``:

```json
"FarField":
{
    "Attributes": [11],
    "ThetaPhis": [[0, 0], [5, 0], ..., [175, 90]]
}
```

The solution uses third-order elements (`"Order": 3`). The simulation takes less than a
minute on a few processes. `rcs_empty.json` is the same configuration on the mesh
without the sphere.

## Analysis and Results

The far field ``r\mathbf{E}_s`` of the scattered field is written to `farfield-rE.csv`
and, since the excitation is a plane wave, the bistatic radar cross section
``\sigma = 4\pi |r\mathbf{E}_s|^2 / |E_0|^2`` in the same directions to
`farfield-rcs.csv`:

```@example include_example
include_example_file("rcs_coated_sphere/rcs_coated_sphere", "farfield-rcs.csv") #hide
```

The monostatic radar cross section, in the backscattering direction ``-\hat{z}``, is
written to `rcs-monostatic.csv`, with its parts along the incident polarization (``x``)
and across it (``y``):

```@example include_example
include_example_file("rcs_coated_sphere/rcs_coated_sphere", "rcs-monostatic.csv") #hide
```

The figure below compares the bistatic radar cross section with the Mie series of the
coated sphere (the PEC core is the boundary condition of the radial functions of the
coating at ``r = a``). The difference is about ``0.2\text{ dB}`` in all directions, and
the far-field error, relative to the maximum of the far field, is
``-35\text{ dB}`` (rms over both planes); the cross-polarized far field on the two
planes, which vanishes by symmetry, is ``-69\text{ dB}`` below the maximum. The
monostatic radar cross section is ``-15.58\text{ dBsm}``, against ``-15.34\text{ dBsm}``
for the Mie series. The difference comes from the discretization and from the absorbing
boundary, which is close to the sphere in this small model: a finer mesh and a larger
domain, or a perfectly matched layer, reduce it.

On the empty box, the far field measures the leakage of the plane wave source (see
[Accuracy](../features/planewave.md#Accuracy)): its maximum is ``3.5 \times 10^{-4}``
V, ``46\text{ dB}`` below the maximum of the far field of the sphere.
